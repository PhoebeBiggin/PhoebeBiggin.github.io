Advanced Interface Prototyping: Project Reflection

Over each stage of the group project, I contributed to the project by providing research, writing and organising administrative tasks to ensure all project members are involved appropriately and equally. For Example: I was responsible for producing the Artefact for the first group assignment. I utilised team planning & brainstorming to plan both the visual design and copy elements of the artefact to highlight the positives and negatives of A.I. driven gallery spaces as a future concept and produced the mock-up for our slide deck. Across all submissions, I was responsible for documenting meeting notes, supplying follow-up communications and deadlines and creating the project documents used for submission (Creating and maintaining a shared Fig Jam for development, establishing & designing templates for the slide deck, documenting supporting material, hosting the Voice-flow account & setup). Additionally, I provided research and written documentation for all three slide decks, including but not limited to: Forecast C A.2.1, Smart Environment Design documentation, Recommendation (A2.2) and the Chatbot Structure Diagram, and Human Design Challenge B.

Across this project, our group made the active choice to avoid the use of A.I. to ensure our outcomes are project specific and authentic to our learning experience. From a positive perspective, the use of Artificial Intelligence for UX and Iterative Prototyping can be significantly beneficial to the brainstorming and evaluative processes. It can greatly speed up the process of creating and executing prototypes: Alike our attempts in A2.2 to make a desktop prototype – A.I. could be able to uplift this process by generating multiple iterations of our prototype, creating broader desktop prototype options to evaluate – overall making more opportunity for a diversity of exploration, whereas our Non A.I. assisted prototype took longer to produce for less broad results. On the other hand, there is a risk of using A.I. systems for iterative prototyping and UX through its limitation of producing generic/repetitive prototypes. In relation to our project, I can see this risk of A.I. not being able to meet the level of specificity and innovative thinking that our prototypes are striving to achieve as part of our ‘futures thinking’. Overall, A.I. can be beneficial to making UX processes more efficient and creating a breadth of results, but always requires a level of human mediation to ensure the outcome is appropriate and effective. This is where our group decided that using A.I. as part of these processes would not be ‘worth’ its faults – as the speed of iteration was not enough of a benefit over the extended level of review it would require.

Over this semester, advanced interface prototyping provided a broader mindset when it comes to design – Through developing a future-focused lens to my design. I have learnt to uplift novel technologies, accessibility and sustainability through my designs within their research, purpose and execution, and have learnt about a myriad of new forms of technologies that may inform our interfaces in our not-so-distant future. Taking this in context as a UX Designer, I feel that the myriad of approaches Advanced Interface Prototyping has taught us (Participatory Design, Futures thinking, Service design & beyond) will allow me to further specialise my approach to User Experience design by considering further stakeholders to the design process and outcome, as well as growth opportunities the industry is likely to experience. I will continue to develop my capabilities as a UX designer by further researching and involving myself in potential new technologies and sustainability measures.

[AI DECLARATION] No A.I. Was used to produce this reflection. It was specifically avoided (Copilot not used) for this reflection and was not used in any form to write, research or finalise its content.
